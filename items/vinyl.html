Vinyl Record

Timeline

  1. 1948 — Columbia Records introduced the 33⅓ rpm long-playing (LP) record.

  2. 1980s — Cassettes and then the compact disc pushed vinyl out of most record stores.

  3. 2008 — Record Store Day began, celebrating independent shops and special vinyl releases.

  4. 2020 — Vinyl sales out-earned CD sales in the US for the first time since the 1980s.

A vinyl record stores sound as a spiral groove pressed into a plastic disc, read by a needle on a turntable. The large sleeve made album art a format of its own, and listening meant sitting with a full side of music.

The cassette tape and the CD player made music more portable, and vinyl became a niche. Its revival has little to do with convenience: people buy records to own something heavy and visible, to display the cover, and to make listening an intentional ritual — the same reason the Polaroid camera came back.
